A GenAI roaming assistant, built with BCG and Meta

A conversational assistant in the self-care app that answers roaming questions and recommends the right roaming pack.

Scale
A three-week pilot across 1M subscribers
Stack
Meta Llama 3.x on AWS Bedrock, OpenAI, REST APIs, the operator's self-care app
Partners
BCG and Meta
Shape
A conversational AI service in the cloud, connected through middleware on the operator's own servers to the app, the customer data and the existing chatbot

The problem

Roaming raises many questions, and the right pack depends on who is asking. The aim was to answer roaming questions in conversation, identify likely roamers, and guide them to activation and the right pack.

What we built

Follow one customer through the system.

  1. Target

    The system works out who is roaming now and who is not.

    Customer context and targeting layer separating active roamers from non-roamers

  2. Ask

    A subscriber types a roaming question in the self-care app.

    Messages pass from the app to middleware on the operator's servers

  3. Add context

    The middleware attaches what is known about this customer.

    Customer context built from customer data held on the operator's servers

  4. Converse

    The message and its context go to the conversational AI in the cloud, which replies.

    Conversational API; Llama 3.x hosted on AWS Bedrock; context, user message and bot message exchanged on each turn

  5. Recommend

    The reply answers the question and suggests the roaming pack that fits, shown in the app.

    Customer context and product information supplied to the bot

  6. Hand over

    A question outside the assistant's scope is passed to the app's existing chatbot.

    Deflection event from the middleware to the existing chatbot

How it was built

  1. Context before conversation. The bot is told who it is talking to, a roamer or not, and what packs exist, so both the answer and the recommendation fit the person.
  2. The AI in the cloud, the customer data at home. The conversational service runs in the cloud. Customer data stays on the operator's own servers, where a middleware layer turns it into the context the assistant needs and sends that with each message.
  3. One middleware, three connections. The app, the customer data and the existing chatbot all meet the AI through the same middleware. The app keeps a single chat surface, and a question outside the assistant's scope is handed to the existing chatbot along the same path.
  4. Llama 3.x on AWS Bedrock. The conversation runs on Meta's model, hosted on Bedrock.
  5. A controlled pilot. Three weeks, 1M subscribers, measured against a control group.

How it was tested

As a three-week pilot across 1M subscribers, against a control group.

My part and the team's
